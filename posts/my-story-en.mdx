---
title: "My story"
date: "2026-11-22"
---

I started programming at the end of 2019, during the school holidays. I was 16 and I picked Java. It went badly: I got stuck and I was close to giving up.

In 2020 one of my teachers mentioned that his brother was a programmer. Then the pandemic came, and I was at home, still stuck. I emailed the teacher to ask for advice. A few days later his brother wrote back with a long email: what he did for a living, what he would learn in my place, and in what order.

I followed it to the letter. I learned web technologies, paid for courses with my savings, and studied up to 16 hours a day until school started again.

I finished technical high school as an electronics technician in 2021. In my first hiring process, the take-home test was a chat app with endpoints that were broken on purpose. I wrote down every problem I found and how I got around it, and I got an offer. In June 2022 I started working as a freelance backend developer.

Today I build data-heavy systems in Python and Go, and I run AC Hub, a platform I built for sim racing championships.

I still work the way I learned: when I'm stuck, I ask, and then I do the work.
